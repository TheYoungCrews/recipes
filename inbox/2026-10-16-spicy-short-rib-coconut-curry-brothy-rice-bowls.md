Brita's (@britacooks) fall-apart short ribs in a red curry coconut broth ladled over rice — the cozy bowl from her "Soups That Don't Suck" series.

## Original
> The IG caption gates the recipe behind a comment, but the full recipe is public on her blog (britacooks.com); this is a faithful decode of the blog version, pressure-cooker method. Her Dutch oven and slow cooker alternates exist on the blog.

**Ingredients**
- 2–2.5 lbs bone-in short ribs
- Kosher salt
- 1 tbsp avocado oil (original says neutral oil; avocado is a guess)
- 3 tbsp red curry paste
- 2 shallots, halved
- 4–5 cloves garlic, smashed
- 4" piece ginger, sliced in coins
- 2 tsp soy sauce or tamari
- 1 tsp fish sauce
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 3–4 cups beef stock
- 1 can (13.5 oz) full-fat coconut milk
- 2 cups cooked white rice
- To finish: Thai basil, sliced green onions, sesame seeds, chili oil (optional)

**Steps**
1. Trim excess fat from the short ribs and season generously with kosher salt on all sides. Set the pressure cooker to sauté, add the oil, and sear the ribs deeply on all sides until well browned. Transfer to a plate.
2. Drain most of the rendered fat, leaving about a tablespoon. Add the red curry paste and cook 2–3 minutes, stirring and scraping up the browned bits, until darkened and fragrant.
3. Add the shallots, garlic, and ginger, stirring into the paste. Return the ribs to the pot, add the soy sauce, fish sauce, and brown sugar, then pour in enough beef stock to mostly submerge the ribs (3–4 cups).
4. Seal and pressure cook on high for 45 minutes.
5. Let the steam release naturally for 15 minutes, then quick-release any remaining pressure.
6. Transfer the ribs to a plate; when cool enough to handle, shred the meat off the bones.
7. Strain the broth, discarding the shallots, garlic, and ginger. Return the broth to the pot, stir in the coconut milk, and adjust with more soy sauce or fish sauce to taste.
8. Divide the rice between bowls, top with shredded short rib, ladle the broth over, and finish with green onions, Thai basil, sesame seeds, and chili oil if you like.
