A cozy skillet dinner with browned bratwurst, sweet Vidalia onion, and warm lentils finished with Dijon mustard, butter, and sherry vinegar. We made it with the cooked bratwurst from Costco's mixed sausage pack and Trader Joe's precooked lentils, and loved it.

## Original

**Ingredients**
- 3–4 fully cooked bratwurst
- 1 package Trader Joe's precooked lentils
- 1 small Vidalia onion, thinly sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- ⅓ cup chicken broth or water, plus more as needed
- 2 teaspoons Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon butter
- 1 teaspoon sherry vinegar, plus more to taste
- Black pepper and salt, to taste (amount not given)
- Brown mustard, for serving (amount not given)
- Optional: 1 small carrot, finely diced; a pinch of thyme; parsley; or a handful of spinach

**Steps**
1. Brown the bratwurst. Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Pat the bratwurst dry and brown gently, turning occasionally, for about 5–7 minutes. Transfer to a plate; they will finish heating with the lentils.
2. Cook the onion. Add the Vidalia onion and optional carrot to the same skillet. Cook for 7–9 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly golden. Add the garlic and optional thyme and cook for 30 seconds.
3. Add the lentils. Gently break apart the lentils in the pan. Stir in the broth, Dijon, and black pepper, scraping up the browned bits.
4. Warm together. Nestle the bratwurst into the lentils. Cover and simmer gently for 5–8 minutes, until everything is hot throughout, following the bratwurst package's heating instructions. Add a splash of broth or water if the lentils look dry. If using spinach, stir it in toward the end to wilt.
5. Finish. Remove from the heat and gently stir the butter and 1 teaspoon sherry vinegar into the lentils. Taste before adding salt, then adjust with more vinegar or mustard if desired. Sprinkle with parsley if using.
6. Serve. Spoon the lentils into shallow bowls and top with bratwurst. Serve brown mustard on the side. Notes: Let the Vidalia onion get soft and lightly golden for sweetness. Sherry vinegar balances the richness without adding more sweetness. Optional accompaniments include pickles, sauerkraut, or a simple green salad.

## Our version
**Grocery & inventory check**

- [x] 1 small Vidalia onion, diced
- [x] 1 small carrot, finely diced
- [x] 2 garlic cloves, minced
- [x] 1 tablespoon olive oil
- [x] Dry vermouth or apple cider (a splash)
- [ ] ⅓ cup chicken broth or water, plus more as needed
- [ ] 2 teaspoons Dijon mustard
- [x] Thyme (a pinch)
- [x] Kale (a handful, chopped, optional)
- [x] 1 tablespoon butter
- [ ] 1 teaspoon sherry vinegar, plus more to taste
- [x] Black pepper and salt, to taste
- [x] Parsley (optional, for serving)
- [x] Sauerkraut (for serving)
- [ ] Brown mustard (for serving)

**Steps**
1. Brown the bratwurst. Slice the bratwurst (3–4) into thick coins and pat dry. Heat the olive oil (1 tablespoon) in a large skillet over medium-high and brown the coins in one layer, turning once, until well crusted, about 5 minutes. Move them to a plate.
2. Cook the vegetables. Turn the heat to medium. Add the onion (1 small, diced) and carrot (1 small, finely diced) to the same skillet and cook 7 to 9 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and lightly golden. Add the garlic (2 cloves, minced) and thyme (a pinch) and cook 30 seconds.
3. Deglaze. Pour in a splash of dry vermouth or apple cider and scrape up the browned bits until it mostly cooks off.
4. Add the lentils. Gently break apart the lentils (1 package) into the pan. Stir in the broth (⅓ cup, plus more as needed), Dijon (2 teaspoons) and black pepper.
5. Warm together. Stir the bratwurst back in, plus the kale (a handful, chopped) if using. Cover and simmer gently 5 to 8 minutes, until everything is hot and the kale is wilted. Add a splash of broth or water if the lentils look dry.
6. Finish. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the butter (1 tablespoon) and sherry vinegar (1 teaspoon). Taste, then add salt and more vinegar or mustard as needed.
7. Serve. Spoon into shallow bowls, top with a spoon of sauerkraut and parsley if using, and serve brown mustard on the side.

**Tips**
- Let the onion get soft and lightly golden for sweetness.
- Sherry vinegar cuts the richness without adding sweetness.
- Pickles or a simple green salad also go well on the side.
