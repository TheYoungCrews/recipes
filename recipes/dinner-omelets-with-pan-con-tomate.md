Katie Button's (@chefkatiebutton) ham and cheddar omelets with pan con tomate, from Spanish Accent.

## Original

**Dinner Omelets with Pan con Tomate**, Katie Button, from Spanish Accent: American Home Recipes with a Spanish Touch (Knopf, 2026). Reprinted at Fathom / Yahoo (https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/breakfast-dinner-squash-dessert-cooking-172628092.html). Serves 4. No prep or cook time given.

- 8 large eggs
- Diamond Crystal kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 4 slices Black Forest deli ham, diced
- 1/2 cup shredded white cheddar cheese
- Extra-virgin olive oil, for the pan
- 4 large slices sourdough boule (see Notes)
- 1 garlic clove, halved
- 2 ripe and juicy red tomatoes, preferably Campari, halved crosswise
- Flaky salt such as Maldon or fleur de sel, for serving
- High-quality extra-virgin olive oil, such as Picual

1. In a small bowl, whisk 2 eggs and season with kosher salt and pepper. Add one-quarter of the ham and 2 tablespoons of the cheddar.
2. Heat a 6-inch nonstick skillet over medium heat and add enough olive oil to coat the bottom of the pan. Pour in the egg mixture and cook until the eggs are set on the bottom, about 2 minutes. Then, gripping the handle, tilt the pan so that the egg begins to roll away from you. Using a fork or chopsticks, tuck the edge into the omelet and let sit until lightly browned, 1 to 2 minutes. Turn the omelet out onto a plate, seam-side down. Repeat to cook 3 more omelets.
3. While the omelets are cooking, toast the bread to your liking. Rub the cut side of the garlic lightly over the toasts (a little goes a long way, just a few light strokes on the toasted bread will work). Rub each toast with the cut half of a tomato so that the juices soak into the bread and you're left with just the skins (these can be drizzled with a bit of olive oil and salt and eaten as a little snack). Sprinkle the toasts with flaky salt and drizzle with high-quality olive oil.
4. Enjoy the omelets with the pan con tomate, either sandwiched between the slices, alongside, or however you feel.

Notes from the book: If you prefer using a baguette, slice it in half lengthwise, then cut each half into large pieces before toasting. When we have this for dinner, I like to put the toaster in the middle of the dining room table so everyone can toast and top to their liking. And if I'm really going for the best pan con tomate ever, I rub the garlic and tomato on both sides of my toast.
