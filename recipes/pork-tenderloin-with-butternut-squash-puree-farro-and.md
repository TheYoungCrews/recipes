Seared, convection-roasted pork tenderloin over butternut squash purée with farro, toasted buttered pecans and crispy onions. Cooked December 2024 and requested again.

## Original
- Pork tenderloin; weight not recorded
- Cooking oil, as needed
- Salt and black pepper

For the purée:
- 1 medium butternut squash
- 1-2 tbsp butter or olive oil, plus a little for roasting
- Salt and black pepper
- Water or broth, as needed to loosen

For serving:
- Farro, quantity as needed
- Toasted buttered pecans
- Crispy onion pieces
- Optional olive oil and fresh herbs for the farro
- Optional sage or rosemary for finishing

1. Prepare the squash first. Split it lengthwise, remove the seeds, lightly oil and season it. Roast at about 375°F until very tender, roughly 40-50 minutes depending on size.
2. Scoop the roasted flesh into a blender or food processor. Blend with 1-2 tbsp butter or olive oil, adding small amounts of water or broth for a smooth purée. Season to taste.
3. Cook farro according to its package instructions. Drain if needed and season with a little olive oil and salt.
4. Season the tenderloin and sear it in a hot, oiled oven-safe skillet until browned all over.
5. Transfer to a 375°F convection oven. Original guidance was about 15-20 minutes after searing, but use a thermometer since the weight wasn't recorded. Reach 145°F internal and rest at least 3 minutes (notes suggested 5-10).
6. Slice the pork. Serve over the warm squash purée with farro alongside. Add toasted buttered pecans and crispy onion pieces.

**Keep:** sear first, then convection-roast; squash purée under or beside the pork; farro, buttered pecans and crispy onions were all part of the meal.

**Uncertain:** pork weight, farro amount and topping quantities not recorded. Cinnamon, nutmeg, maple syrup, sage and rosemary were only suggestions, with no clear record they went into the purée.
