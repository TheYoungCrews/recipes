A small, classic Wellington with store-bought pastry, mushrooms and prosciutto. Recipe by Elise Bauer, Simply Recipes.

## Original
**Ingredients**
- 1 lb thick beef tenderloin
- Salt and black pepper, to taste (amount not given)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp prepared English yellow mustard
- 1 lb mushrooms
- 4 thin slices Parma ham or prosciutto
- 1 sheet thawed puff pastry, 7–8.5 oz
- 2 egg yolks, beaten
- Flaky salt for topping (amount not given; optional)
- Flour for rolling (amount not given)

**Steps**
1. Season and sear beef in oil, about 2–3 minutes per side. Cool and brush with mustard.
2. Finely chop mushrooms; cook 10–15 minutes until dry, then cool. Layer ham and mushrooms on plastic wrap; roll around beef and chill 20 minutes.
3. Roll out pastry. Remove plastic, enclose beef, seal with yolk and trim excess. Set seam down, brush with yolk and chill 5–10 minutes.
4. Heat oven to 400°F. Score lightly, brush again and add flaky salt. Bake 25–35 minutes; the source specifies 125–130°F for medium-rare. Rest 10 minutes, then slice. That source temperature is below the USDA safe minimum; see the cooking guidance below.

## Our version
**Grocery & inventory check**
- [ ] Beef tenderloin (1 lb, one evenly thick, trimmed center-cut roast)
- [x] Salt (to taste; amount not given)
- [x] Black pepper (to taste; amount not given)
- [x] Olive oil (2 tbsp)
- [ ] Prepared English yellow mustard (2 tbsp; condiment, not mustard powder)
- [ ] Mushrooms (1 lb; cremini work well)
- [ ] Prosciutto (4 thin slices)
- [ ] Puff pastry (1 sheet, 7–8.5 oz; thawed but cold)
- [x] Egg yolks (2, beaten)
- [ ] Flaky salt (optional; amount not given)
- [ ] Flour (for rolling; amount not given)

**Before starting**
Allow 2–2.5 hours for a first attempt, plus pastry thawing if needed. The source's 105 minutes includes 40 minutes cooling/chilling in addition to 20 minutes prep and 45 minutes cooking. Serves four small portions, or two to three generously with sides. Ask the butcher for one roast, not four steaks. Have an instant-read thermometer, wide skillet, plastic wrap, rolling pin and lined baking sheet ready. All-butter pastry is a good purchase preference, not a requirement.

**Steps**
1. Thaw the puff pastry (1 sheet, 7–8.5 oz) according to its package, keeping it cold. Pat the beef tenderloin (1 lb) dry and season with salt and black pepper. Heat the olive oil (2 tbsp) in a skillet; brown every surface, about 2–3 minutes per side, without trying to cook the center. Cool, then brush with the prepared English yellow mustard (2 tbsp).
2. Finely chop the mushrooms (1 lb) with a processor or knife. Cook in a wide pan for 10–15 minutes, stirring, until all released liquid has evaporated. Cool completely. Wet mushrooms or warm filling will soften the pastry.
3. Overlap the prosciutto (4 thin slices) on plastic wrap, spread with the cooled mushrooms (1 lb, cooked), and place the mustard-coated beef tenderloin (1 lb) on top. Use the wrap to roll everything tightly; twist the ends and refrigerate for 20 minutes.
4. Dust the counter with flour and roll the puff pastry (1 sheet, 7–8.5 oz) just large enough to enclose the log. Remove and discard ALL plastic wrap. Wrap the log in pastry, sealing edges with some of the beaten egg yolks (2 total). Trim thick overlaps, place seam down on the lined sheet, and brush with more yolk, reserving some for a final coat. Chill 5–10 minutes.
5. Heat a conventional oven to 400°F (about 200°C). Score the pastry lightly without cutting through. Brush with the remaining beaten egg yolks (from the 2 total) and sprinkle with optional flaky salt.
6. Bake for about 25–35 minutes, starting thermometer checks around 25 minutes. Measure the center of the beef, not the pastry. For the USDA safe endpoint, reach 145°F BEFORE removing from the oven; allow extra time as needed and loosely shield the pastry with foil if it browns too quickly. The clock alone cannot establish doneness.
7. Rest for 10 minutes before cutting into roughly 1-inch slices. Serve with simple potatoes and green beans.

**Temperature and timing**
Elise Bauer specifies a traditional medium-rare pull temperature of 125–130°F. This is below the USDA safe minimum and is not the safety target. FoodSafety.gov specifies 145°F for beef roasts, measured before removal from heat, followed by at least a 3-minute rest. Follow that safe endpoint, especially for pregnant or immunocompromised diners. Reference: https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures

**Make it easier**
Cook the mushrooms up to two days ahead, cool promptly and refrigerate airtight. Thaw the pastry according to its package the day before if helpful. Sear, assemble and bake the Wellington on cooking day; serve it freshly baked. Keep filling and pastry cold, mushrooms dry, and pastry overlaps thin. This is a weekend project; Crews has not tested it yet.
