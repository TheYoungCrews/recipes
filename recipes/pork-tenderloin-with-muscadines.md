Seared, oven-roasted pork tenderloin with blistered muscadines, sage and a quick Dijon-butter pan sauce. Georgia's fall grape. Virginia Willis for the AJC.

## Original
- 3 1/2 cups (20 ounces) muscadine or scuppernong grapes
- 1 pork tenderloin (1 1/4 pounds), trimmed
- Coarse kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tablespoon canola oil
- 4 sage leaves, more for garnish
- 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
- 1 tablespoon firm unsalted butter

1. Heat oven to 425 degrees.
2. Rinse and dry the grapes, halve them and remove the seeds.
3. Season the pork with salt and pepper.
4. Heat oil in a large ovenproof skillet over medium-high. Sear pork on all sides until browned, 3 to 5 minutes. Add grapes and sage. Roast until just barely pink in the center and 145 degrees, 12 to 14 minutes. Rest on a cutting board.
5. Put the skillet over medium heat (handle is hot), stir in mustard, remove from heat and swirl in the butter to melt slowly. Slice pork 1/2 inch thick. Spoon grapes and sauce onto plates, top with pork, garnish with sage.

Serves 4.

**Steps**
1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Halve and seed the grapes.
2. Season the pork, then sear it in the oil in an ovenproof skillet over medium-high, 3-5 minutes all over.
3. Add the grapes and sage and roast 12-14 minutes, to 145°F. Rest the pork.
4. Back on medium heat, stir the mustard into the pan, pull it off the heat and swirl in the butter.
5. Slice the pork 1/2 inch thick and serve over the grapes and sauce with more sage.
