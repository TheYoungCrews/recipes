The Georgia roadside snack: peanuts in the shell simmered for hours in salty brine. Green peanuts are in season early fall. Matt and Ted Lee for Southern Living.

## Original
- 1 1/2 cups kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
- 2 lbs. raw peanuts in the shell or 3 lbs. green peanuts

1. Put 2 gallons water in a 10- to 12-quart stockpot with 1/2 cup salt; stir to dissolve. Add raw peanuts, submerge with a plate, soak 8 hours or overnight. (Skip for green peanuts; optional if short on time.)
2. Drain; add 2 gallons water and 1 cup salt. Note the water level. Bring to a boil over high, reduce to low and simmer covered until as soft as roasted chestnuts or softer, 5 to 8 hours (2 to 3 for green), topping up water to stay within an inch of the original level.
3. After 3 hours (1 for green), sample for texture and salt. Crunchy: cook more. Under-salted: add salt by 1/4 cup. Too salty: swap some brine for fresh water and wait an hour. Sample hourly until yielding and as salty as a good pickle.
4. Cool in the pot 1 hour (20 minutes for green). Drain and eat, or refrigerate in the shell 7 to 10 days or freeze several months.

## Our version
- [ ] raw peanuts in the shell (2 lb) or green peanuts (3 lb)
- [ ] kosher salt (1 1/2 cups, plus more to taste)

**Steps**
1. For raw peanuts, soak overnight in 2 gallons water with 1/2 cup salt, weighed down with a plate. Skip for green peanuts.
2. Drain, add 2 gallons fresh water and 1 cup salt, mark the water line, and boil, then simmer covered 5-8 hours (2-3 for green), topping up water as it drops.
3. Taste from hour 3 (hour 1 for green) and every hour after; adjust salt by 1/4 cup or dilute with fresh water.
4. Cool in the brine 1 hour (20 minutes for green), then drain. Keeps 7-10 days in the fridge or months frozen.
