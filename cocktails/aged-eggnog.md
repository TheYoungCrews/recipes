Alton Brown's aged eggnog: rum, cognac and bourbon blended with yolks, sugar and nutmeg, aged in two phases for about 90 days. Strong, rich, and a great holiday batch if you start by mid-October.

## Original
Phase 1:
- 12 large eggs (yolks only)
- 1 cup Jamaican rum (Appleton Estate 43% ABV)
- 1 cup cognac (Ferrand 40% ABV)
- 1 cup bourbon (Old Forester 50% ABV)
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1 pound sugar
- 2/3 teaspoon nutmeg, grated on a rasp

Phase 2:
- 1 cup whole milk
- 1 pint half-and-half
- 1 pint heavy cream
- 1/3 teaspoon nutmeg, grated on a rasp

Hardware: immersion (stick) blender.

1. Separate the eggs and put the yolks in a 2-quart container. Save the whites for something else (souffle, macaroons, angel food cake, pavlova).
2. Combine the rum, cognac and bourbon in a vessel with a pour spout.
3. Add the milk, sugar and nutmeg to the yolks and blend with a stick blender until smooth, keeping the head submerged to avoid foam.
4. With the blender running, slowly pour in the spirits until fully combined.
5. Lid it and refrigerate 30 days (two 2-quart jars with tight lids are easier to stash). Shake now and then.
6. Phase 2: recombine into a vessel that holds at least 3 quarts. With the blender on medium, slowly add the milk, half-and-half, cream and nutmeg until smooth.
7. Lid it and refrigerate another 50-60 days, shaking occasionally.
8. Serve straight from the fridge. Unopened jars keep at least 6 months.

The first phase stays above 20% ABV, which cuts any salmonella by over 99%. For more protection, heat-treat the eggs first.
